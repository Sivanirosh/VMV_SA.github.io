Sulakshana Free Education Centre is the central education initiative of VMV Udhavikkaram. It exists because VMV believes that a capable, self-thinking and self-sufficient society can be built only through education.

## From One Classroom to a Community Centre

The centre began with one classroom and 20 students. Under the guidance of Teacher Ashok, who has provided free mathematics education since 2005, it has grown into a wider learning centre for Pungudutivu.

Today, Sulakshana Free Education Centre serves 167 students from Grade 1 to Grade 11. Students come from all twelve wards of Pungudutivu, and the centre now operates with five classrooms and nine teachers.

## Education Without Regional Bias

VMV Udhavikkaram grew from the Vallan, Mavuththidal and Veeramamlai Helping Hand initiative. To make clear that its work is for all twelve wards without regional bias, VMV incorporated Sulakshana Education Centre and made education its central long-term path.

## Technology and Reading

To improve students’ technological knowledge, computer classes began on 26 January 2024 through the support of diaspora donors.

VMV donors also sent 1,800 books to establish Pungudi Arivagam, the library serving the education centre. The library has expanded and continues to benefit students.

## Student Welfare

VMV’s work extends beyond classroom teaching. It includes monthly educational scholarships, laptops for poverty-level students, bicycles to reduce transport barriers, and first-aid training for students.

A child welfare savings scheme also began in April 2022, with Rs. 25,000 deposited for each of 25 children from the villages.

## Future Planning

VMV plans to organise classes so more students across Pungudutivu can benefit from the centre. For Advanced Level students who spend significant time travelling to Jaffna, VMV plans to support vocational training guidance and seminars.

Transport remains a major barrier. Vehicle support for students travelling from places such as Irruppiddy, Kurikadduwan, Perunkadu and Madathuveli would help sustain their interest in education.

VMV also recognises that creating separate education centres in every ward can waste scarce manpower and donor resources when centres lack long-term administration. The focus is therefore to strengthen a capable, well-managed centre that can serve Pungudutivu as a whole.
